Blender.io and Sinbad.io were **bitcoin mixing** services that, in the U.S.
government’s charging narrative, let users send bitcoin to designated recipients
in a way designed to hide the source of funds.

## What the public record establishes

According to the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Northern District of Georgia
(**10 January 2025**):

- A grand jury indicted **Roman Vitalyevich Ostapenko**, **Alexander Evgenievich
  Oleynik**, and **Anton Vyachlavovich Tarasov** for involvement in operating
  **Blender.io** and **Sinbad.io**.
- Blender.io operated from about **2018 to 2022** and was marketed with a “no
  logs” / no-registration pitch (as described in the release).
- After Blender shut down, **Sinbad.io** offered similar bitcoin concealment
  services.
- On **27 November 2023**, Sinbad.io was taken down by law enforcement action.
- Ostapenko and Oleynik were arrested on **1 December 2024**.

An indictment **alleges** crimes. Legal status is **charged**; case status is
**open** pending public plea, trial, or dismissal outcomes.

## Why it matters for Bitcoin history

These services continue the post-2017 **mixer enforcement arc** (Fog, Helix,
ChipMixer, Samourai). Treasury sanctions and criminal charging often run in
parallel on mixers; this file records the **criminal charging** thread from
NDGA’s primary release.
