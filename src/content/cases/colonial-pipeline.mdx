Colonial Pipeline’s May 2021 ransomware incident became a mainstream
illustration of **critical-infrastructure risk** and of Bitcoin as a ransomware
payment rail — followed, unusually quickly, by a public U.S. seizure of most of
the reported ransom.

## What the DOJ release states

On **7 June 2021**, the Department of Justice announced that it had seized
**63.7 bitcoins**, then valued at approximately **$2.3 million**. According to
that release:

- On or about **7 May 2021**, Colonial Pipeline suffered a ransomware attack
  associated with a group known as **DarkSide** and took portions of its
  infrastructure out of operation.
- Colonial Pipeline reported to the FBI that it had paid a ransom of
  approximately **75 bitcoin**.
- By reviewing the Bitcoin public ledger, investigators tracked transfers and
  identified about **63.7 BTC** at an address for which the FBI obtained the
  private key.
- A seizure warrant was authorized by a U.S. magistrate judge in the Northern
  District of California.

DOJ characterized the seized bitcoin as proceeds traceable to a computer
intrusion and property involved in money laundering **as alleged** in the
supporting affidavit.

## Technical analysis

From the DOJ June 2021 seizure announcement (primary):

### Ransomware payment rail

- Victim reported paying approximately **75 BTC** to DarkSide after a May 2021
  intrusion that forced operational shutdown of pipeline systems.
- Payment is a standard **extortion settlement on Bitcoin mainnet** — transparent
  ledger, not a privacy coin.

### Tracing and key control

- Investigators **reviewed the Bitcoin public ledger**, followed transfers from
  the ransom payment path, and identified ~**63.7 BTC** at a specific address.
- The FBI obtained the **private key** for that address (DOJ: “rough equivalent
  of a password needed to access assets”), then seized under a magistrate-
  authorized warrant (N.D. Cal.).
- Technical implication: funds remained in UTXOs the government could spend once
  it controlled the key — recovery depends on **timing** (coins not yet fully
  cashed out through mixers/exchanges) plus **chain analysis**, not on reversing
  encryption of the pipeline systems.

### What this does *not* document

- The DOJ release does not publish a full technical malware reverse-engineering
  of DarkSide binaries; this file stays with the **payment-and-recovery** graph.

## Legal status on this site

This case file’s legal status is **unresolved** at the person-level: the June
2021 DOJ release focuses on **seizure of funds**, not on naming and convicting
specific DarkSide operators in that same announcement. “Partial” case status
reflects a major recovery without a single closed prosecution narrative in the
cited primary source.

## Why it matters for Bitcoin history

- Showed policymakers treating ransomware-for-bitcoin as a national-security
  and infrastructure issue, not only a corporate IT failure.
- Demonstrated that **speed of reporting** plus chain analysis can enable
  recovery of a large fraction of a ransom while coins remain accessible to
  investigators.
- Reinforced that BTC payments are **traceable**, even when recipients use
  multiple transfers.
