BTC-e was, in the U.S. government’s description, a large virtual-currency
exchange that processed enormous transaction volume while allegedly catering to
cybercrime proceeds. Its operator **Alexander Vinnik** became one of the most
significant money-laundering defendants in Bitcoin’s first decade of enforcement.

## Indictment era (2017)

In July 2017, the Northern District of California announced a multi-count
indictment against **Vinnik** and **BTC-e**, and reported Vinnik’s arrest in
Greece. The same announcement described FinCEN civil penalties of **$110 million**
against BTC-e and **$12 million** against Vinnik for AML violations.

At that stage, charges were **allegations**. This archive records them as such
in the timeline.

## Guilty plea (2024)

On **3 May 2024**, DOJ announced that Vinnik pleaded guilty to **conspiracy to
commit money laundering** related to operating BTC-e from **2011 to 2017**.
Agency releases state that:

- BTC-e was among the world’s largest virtual currency exchanges in that period;
- it moved **over $9 billion** worth of transactions (agency figure);
- Vinnik was responsible for a loss amount of at least **$121 million**.

Legal status on this site is therefore **convicted** (guilty plea). Case status
remains **partial** because sentencing and any later executive or diplomatic
disposition may not be fully reflected in the cited plea releases alone.

## Links to other cases

Prosecutors have long tied BTC-e’s user base to proceeds of other crimes,
including activity connected to **Mt. Gox** theft narratives in charging
materials. Those connections are **prosecutorial allegations** where not
admitted in a plea; see the related Mt. Gox theft charges file.
