Hydra was, in the Justice Department’s 2022 description, the world’s **largest
and longest-running darknet market** at the time of its coordinated U.S.–German
shutdown. Cryptocurrency — including bitcoin — was central to how the market
settled trade.

## What the public record establishes

According to DOJ’s **5 April 2022** announcement and the Attorney General’s
digital-assets report:

- Hydra was seized as the world’s largest and longest-running darknet market.
- German **Bundeskriminalamt** seized servers and cryptocurrency wallets
  containing about **$25 million** worth of **bitcoin**, with U.S. coordination.
- Until shutdown, the marketplace had received approximately **$5.2 billion**
  in cryptocurrency (agency figure).
- In **2021**, Hydra accounted for an estimated **80%** of darknet
  market-related cryptocurrency transactions (agency estimate).
- DOJ materials describe criminal charges against **Dmitry Olegovich Pavlov**,
  alleged server administrator since the site’s creation around November 2015.

An indictment or charging announcement **alleges** crimes. Legal status on this
file is **charged** for the named defendant thread; case status is **partial**
(infrastructure seizure is public; full person-level outcomes may continue
elsewhere).

## Why it matters for Bitcoin history

Hydra marks the late dark-market era after Silk Road and AlphaBay: extreme
concentration of darknet crypto volume, in-house mixing / cash-out services in
agency descriptions, and a model of **cross-border infrastructure seizure**
rather than a single U.S. jury trial narrative.
