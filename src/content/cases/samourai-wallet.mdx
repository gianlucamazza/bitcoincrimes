Samourai Wallet sits at the center of the U.S. debate over **Bitcoin privacy
tools** versus **money transmission** rules. This file records what SDNY put on
the public record after plea and sentencing — not a policy argument for or
against privacy software.

## What the public record establishes

According to the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of New York:

- **Keonne Rodriguez** (CEO/co-founder) and **William Lonergan Hill** (CTO/co-founder)
  operated Samourai Wallet, described as a cryptocurrency mixer.
- They **pleaded guilty** on **30 July 2025** to conspiracy to operate a money
  transmitting business knowing the business transmitted crime proceeds.
- SDNY states they knowingly transmitted **over $237 million** in criminal
  proceeds (sentencing release) and agreed to forfeit **$237,832,360.55** (plea
  release).
- **Rodriguez** was sentenced to **five years** (6 Nov 2025); **Hill** to
  **four years** (19 Nov 2025).

## Why it matters for Bitcoin history

The case extends the enforcement line from darknet mixers (Fog, Helix) into
**wallet software marketed for privacy**, under money-transmitting and proceeds
theories. It is a primary data point for anyone writing about Bitcoin privacy
and U.S. criminal law after 2024–2025.
