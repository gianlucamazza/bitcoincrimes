Bitcoin Fog was a long-running **cryptocurrency mixer** (also called a tumbler):
users sent bitcoin in and received different coins out, obscuring simple chain
analysis. U.S. prosecutors argued it was, in practice, a **money-laundering
service** for darknet crime proceeds.

## Conviction and sentence

According to the Department of Justice and the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the
District of Columbia:

- **Roman Sterlingov** operated Bitcoin Fog from **2011 through 2021**.
- In **March 2024**, after a roughly one-month trial, a jury found him guilty of
  money laundering conspiracy, money laundering, operating an unlicensed money
  transmitting business, and a related D.C. money-transmission offense.
- The jury also granted forfeiture of specific assets, including **over about
  1,354 bitcoin** held in a Bitcoin Fog wallet (plus other seized balances),
  per the USADC conviction release.
- In **November 2024**, he was sentenced to **12 years and six months**
  (**150 months**) in prison. DOJ’s sentencing release states he laundered
  **over $400 million** in criminal proceeds through the service (agency figure
  at sentencing).

## Context

Mixers sit at a policy and legal fault line: privacy tooling versus facilitation
of crime. This archive records **what courts and agencies established in this
case**, not a general ban on privacy technology.

DOJ materials in the separate Bitfinex laundering case also list Bitcoin Fog
among mixers used for some stolen funds — another illustration of how mixer
services appear in multi-case tracing narratives.

## Reading notes

- The **$400 million** figure is an **agency sentencing characterization**, not
  an independent audit reproduced here.
- BTC forfeiture (~1,354) is as reported by USADC at conviction.
