Mt. Gox is central to early Bitcoin history: a dominant exchange that later
collapsed, leaving customers without access to funds. This case file focuses on
**U.S. criminal charges** about a **2011 intrusion and multi-year theft**, not on
every civil, bankruptcy, or foreign proceeding related to the exchange.

## What U.S. prosecutors allege (2023 unsealing)

According to the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of New York
and the Department of Justice:

- **Alexey Bilyuchenko** and **Aleksandr Verner** were charged with conspiring to
  launder approximately **647,000 bitcoins** from the **2011 hack of Mt. Gox**.
- Prosecutors allege that from about **September 2011** the defendants and
  co-conspirators accessed wallet infrastructure and moved customer bitcoin to
  addresses they controlled, continuing through at least **May 2014**, and that
  the volume represented the vast majority of customer BTC.
- Bilyuchenko was **separately** charged in the Northern District of California
  with conspiring with **Alexander Vinnik** to operate **BTC-e** (see related case).

A charging announcement **alleges** crimes. Unless and until conviction, the
legal status on this site remains **charged**, and named defendants remain
presumed innocent under U.S. law.

## What this file does **not** claim

- It does **not** restate Japanese criminal or civil outcomes for Mark Karpelès
  or other parties without citing those primary records here. Japanese
  proceedings and the exchange’s civil rehabilitation / creditor process are
  **out of this file’s scope** until primary sources are added at the same
  standard.
- It does **not** treat the 2014 insolvency alone as a single “hack conviction.”
- Dollar valuations of 647,000 BTC vary enormously by date; this file lists the
  **BTC figure as charged**, without inventing a single USD headline.

## Why it matters

The Mt. Gox loss shaped custody standards, proof-of-reserves debates, and
years of recovery litigation. The 2023 U.S. charges reopened the **criminal
attribution** question for a large share of missing coins, connecting the theft
narrative to later laundering venues such as BTC-e in prosecutors’ account.
