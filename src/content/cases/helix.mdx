Helix was a darknet **bitcoin mixer**: customers sent BTC in and received
different coins out, obscuring simple chain analysis. U.S. prosecutors treated
it as a money-laundering service tied to darknet market proceeds.

## What DOJ says happened

According to Department of Justice and U.S. Attorney’s Office (D.C.) releases:

- **Larry Dean Harmon** operated Helix (connected to the darknet search engine
  **Grams** in agency descriptions).
- From **2014 to 2017**, Helix processed transactions involving **over $300
  million** worth of cryptocurrency (agency framing at the time of the
  transactions).
- Later forfeiture materials state Helix processed at least approximately
  **354,468 bitcoin** for customers.
- Harmon **pleaded guilty** on **18 August 2021** to conspiracy to commit money
  laundering.
- On **15 November 2024**, he was **sentenced to three years** (36 months) in
  prison.
- In **January 2026**, DOJ announced final forfeiture title over more than
  **$400 million** in assets tied to Helix (agency-reported value).

## Why it matters

Together with Bitcoin Fog, Helix is a primary-source illustration of how
**mixers** became a dedicated enforcement target — not only marketplace admins.
The case also shows multi-year timelines from operation to plea, sentence, and
forfeiture closure.

## Reading notes

- BTC processed (**~354,468**) and USD (**~$300M** at transaction-time framing)
  are **agency figures**, not independent audits.
- The **$400M** forfeiture figure is a **2026 announcement valuation** of seized
  assets, not the same metric as 2014–2017 throughput.
