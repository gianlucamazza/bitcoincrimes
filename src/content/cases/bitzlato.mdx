Bitzlato was a global cryptocurrency exchange that U.S. prosecutors cast as a
**high-risk on/off-ramp** for darknet and ransomware proceeds — especially
traffic linked to **Hydra Market**.

## What the public record establishes

According to DOJ and EDNY:

- In **January 2023**, **Anatoly Legkodymov** (also known in materials as
  Anatolii Legkodymov / “Gandalf” / “Tolik”) was charged with conducting an
  **unlicensed money transmitting business**.
- Charging materials state Hydra users exchanged more than **$700 million** in
  cryptocurrency with Bitzlato (directly or via intermediaries) until Hydra’s
  2022 shutdown, and that Bitzlato received more than **$15 million** in
  ransomware proceeds.
- In **December 2023**, DOJ announced he **pleaded guilty**, agreed to
  **dissolve Bitzlato**, and to release claims over about **$23 million** in
  seized Bitzlato assets.

Legal status is **convicted** (guilty plea). Case status is **partial** if
sentencing or distribution threads are not fully covered in the cited plea
release.

## Why it matters for Bitcoin history

Bitzlato is a bridge case between **dark markets** (Hydra) and **exchange AML /
MSB** enforcement: not a marketplace itself, but a venue prosecutors said
criminals used to move value — including bitcoin and other crypto — at scale.
