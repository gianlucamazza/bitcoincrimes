AlphaBay was a major darknet marketplace that, in the Justice Department’s
2017 description, had become the largest online “dark market” when U.S. and
international partners seized it.

## What the public record establishes

According to DOJ’s July 2017 announcement:

- **Alexandre Cazes** was charged as creator and administrator of AlphaBay.
- He was arrested in Thailand on **5 July 2017**; DOJ reported he died in
  custody on **12 July 2017**.
- The marketplace operated for over two years on the dark web and was seized
  in a multi-agency action.
- Civil forfeiture actions targeted assets attributed to Cazes and related
  holdings, including cryptocurrency seized by FBI and DEA (agency description).

Because Cazes was never tried to a U.S. verdict, this file’s legal status for
the founder thread is **charged**, not convicted. Separate defendants who
worked on AlphaBay were later **convicted and sentenced** (Herrell, Wheeler),
which is why case status is **partial**.

## Why it matters for Bitcoin history

AlphaBay sits in the post–Silk Road market cycle: large multi-vendor dark
markets continued to use cryptocurrency (including Bitcoin) as a core payment
rail until major coordinated takedowns. Enforcement shifted from single-site
stories to repeated marketplace migrations and parallel infrastructure
(mixers, forums, staff roles).

## Reading notes

- Do not treat the 2017 shutdown announcement as a conviction of Cazes.
- Staff sentences are distinct criminal judgments; they do not convert the
  founder’s charges into a trial verdict.
- Bitcoin amounts for site volume are **not** invented here without a clear
  primary figure in the cited releases.
