In late July 2026, bitcoin self-custody users of **COLDCARD** hardware wallets
(made by **Coinkite**) faced an active theft wave tied not to a broken
blockchain, but to **weak wallet seeds** produced by affected firmware.

## What the public record establishes

### Vendor and independent technical primary sources

According to Coinkite’s security advisory and technical deep dive, and Block’s
engineering analysis (all published around **30 July 2026**, with Coinkite
updates through **1 August 2026**):

- Seed generation on certain firmware versions did **not** deliver the intended
  hardware-RNG strength.
- A March **2021** integration path caused `ngu.random` to use MicroPython’s
  **software PRNG fallback** (Yasmarang) instead of the board’s STM32 hardware
  RNG path Coinkite believed it was using.
- **Mk2/Mk3** firmware **4.0.1 through 4.1.9**: Coinkite states funds are at
  risk when the seed was created **without** at least **50** fair independent
  private dice rolls **and** without a **strong, unique BIP-39 passphrase**.
- **Mk4, Q, and Mk5** seeds generated before fixed releases are also affected
  (Coinkite: about **72 bits** of entropy vs expected **128**).
- Coinkite estimates ~**40 bits** effective search space for Mk2/Mk3 under its
  current attack assumptions (preliminary).
- **TAPSIGNER, OPENDIME, and SATSCARD** are **not** affected (different
  codebases).
- Fixed firmware versions were released for all affected tracks; **updating
  firmware does not repair an existing seed** — a new seed and migration are
  required unless the dice-entropy exception applies.

Block’s write-up independently describes the same class of bug (incorrect
`MICROPY_HW_ENABLE_RNG` check, Yasmarang fallback, and limited 32-bit reseed on
later models) and states active exploitation was underway at publication.

### Thefts

Widespread reporting describes large **on-chain sweeps** of addresses believed
linked to weak Coldcard-generated wallets beginning **30 July 2026**. Media and
research firms have published evolving BTC totals (hundreds to over a thousand
BTC in early estimates). **Coinkite’s primary advisories do not publish an
official stolen-funds total**; this archive therefore **does not freeze a single
USD/BTC loss figure** as a vendor fact.

No public law-enforcement charging document against a named thief is cited here
as of last review. Legal status is **unresolved**; case status is **open**.

## Why it matters for Bitcoin history

Hardware wallets are marketed as the gold standard of **self-custody**. This
incident shows that:

1. **Implementation bugs** in open-source firmware can still collapse entropy.
2. Attackers need not steal devices if seeds are offline-guessable.
3. “Air-gapped” signing does not help if the **seed was born weak**.
4. Operational mitigations (dice entropy, strong passphrases, multisig with
   independent secure cosigners) matter — and firmware updates do not rewrite
   history for old seeds.

## Reading notes

- This is **theft enabled by a vulnerability**, not a court case with a
  convicted defendant in the sources above.
- Do not equate “Coinkite bug” with “Coinkite stole funds.”
- Do not treat media dollar totals as fixed; they change as trackers update.
- Multisig composed **only** of vulnerable COLDCARD-generated keys does not
  remove the issue (Block notes a quorum of secure devices is needed).
- Prefer Coinkite + Block links over social-media chain analysis when stating
  technical facts.
