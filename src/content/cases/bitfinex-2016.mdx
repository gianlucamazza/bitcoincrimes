The 2016 Bitfinex theft is among the largest exchange losses denominated in
bitcoin. Years later it became a landmark **tracing and recovery** case when
U.S. authorities seized a large share of the stolen coins and prosecuted the
people they said controlled the laundering.

## What DOJ says happened

According to Department of Justice releases and court documents:

1. In **August 2016**, Ilya Lichtenstein gained access to Bitfinex’s network and
   caused **119,754 BTC** to leave the exchange across more than 2,000
   transactions.
2. Lichtenstein and Heather Morgan then engaged in an extended effort to
   **launder** the proceeds (mixers, layered transfers, accounts, and related
   techniques described in DOJ materials).
3. In **February 2022**, authorities announced their arrests and the seizure of
   over **$3.6 billion** in cryptocurrency linked to the hack (value as stated
   by DOJ at the time). An investigative affidavit describes seizing about
   **94,636 BTC** using private keys recovered from Lichtenstein’s files.
4. In **August 2023**, both pleaded guilty to money-laundering conspiracies.
5. In **November 2024**, Lichtenstein was sentenced to **five years** in prison.

## Historical context

The case sits at the intersection of three themes in Bitcoin history:

- **Exchange custody risk** — large hot/cold wallet failures shape market trust.
- **Traceability** — multi-year laundering still left a graph law enforcement
  could follow with warrants and keys.
- **Scale of seizures** — DOJ described the recovery among its largest
  financial seizures.

## Reading notes

- Early 2022 arrest materials used **“alleged”** language; post-plea and
  sentencing releases state facts as established in the criminal case. This
  file’s legal status is **convicted** based on the guilty pleas and
  Lichtenstein’s sentence.
- USD figures are **point-in-time agency valuations**; BTC amounts are more
  stable for comparison across years.
- Victim remediation and civil distribution processes may continue after
  criminal sentencing; those threads are only noted as **partial** case status.
