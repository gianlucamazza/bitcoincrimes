# Bitcoin Crimes — brand & voice

## Identity

| Element | Value |
|---------|--------|
| **Name** | Bitcoin Crimes |
| **Descriptor** | Public Record Archive |
| **Tagline** | Bitcoin’s criminal history, with sources you can open. |
| **Promise** | Fewer cases. Harder sources. Status labeled. Dollars dated. |
| **Audience** | Journalists, researchers, educators, careful readers |
| **Not** | Law enforcement, true-crime entertainment, address-risk tool, legal advice |

## Positioning

An independent **public-record archive** of high-profile cases where **Bitcoin was central**. We document what courts and agencies put on the record — not rumor, not undated “billion-dollar” headlines, not guilt by association.

**Neutral on the technology. Strict on the record.**

## Voice

**Persona:** investigative archive editor — calm, precise, footnote-first.

### Do

- “According to SDNY…”, “the jury convicted”, “pled guilty to”, “the government alleged”
- Prefer **BTC** amounts; every **USD** with an as-of date
- State **legal status** explicitly (charged ≠ convicted)
- When only charges exist: presumption of innocence
- Admit coverage limits (U.S. agency records dominate v1)
- Short sentences; concrete nouns; sourced numbers

### Don’t

- “Kingpin”, “mastermind”, “evil empire” (unless quoting a source)
- Undated dollar claims or invented totals
- Imply guilt from a timeline alone
- Developer jargon on public pages (“build fails”, “source_id”, “Zod”)
- “v1” in reader-facing copy (use “current coverage” / “this archive”)
- Claim affiliation with any government agency
- Promise completeness of “all crypto crime”

## Microcopy patterns

| Surface | Pattern |
|---------|---------|
| Hero | Tagline + one-sentence scope + trust promise |
| Case summary | Neutral, ≤~320 chars, one narrative thread |
| Amount labels | What + who reported (e.g. “Seized (DOJ, Jun 2021)”) |
| Related | Why linked, tied to a public record when possible |
| Disclaimer | “Public records. Status labeled. Not legal advice.” |

## Visual tone

Dark paper, restrained amber accent, serif titles, mono for amounts/dates. **Investigative journal**, not casino, not blood-orange crypto cliché.

## Tagline lock-in (use consistently)

> Bitcoin’s criminal history, with sources you can open.

Short form (nav/footer): **Public Record Archive**
